DUI Bus Crash Downtown: Pedestrian Critical, Multiple Aboard Injured

Alder Creek Transit District Owl-route bus struck a woman at a South Main Street stop early Saturday after clipping a City light standard, police say. Operator arrested on suspicion of DUI.

By Rivergate Sentinel Staff

Published 10/18/2025, 6:18 a.m. PT · Updated 10/18/2025, 2:42 p.m. PT

An overnight collision involving an Alder Creek Transit District (ACTD) Owl-service bus left a pedestrian in critical condition and sent injured passengers to area hospitals early Saturday, authorities said. The bus operator was taken into custody on suspicion of driving under the influence.

According to the Rivergate Police Department, the collision was reported at approximately 2 a.m. on South Main Street in downtown Rivergate, near the intersection with Third Avenue. Officers responding to the scene said the northbound bus, operating on one of ACTD's late-night Owl routes, first struck a City-owned street-light standard on the west side of Main, knocking out power to the fixture and darkening the intersection. The bus then struck a woman who had been waiting at the adjacent curbside bus stop.

The pedestrian, described by police as a local retail worker in her 30s, was airlifted from the scene in critical condition to a regional trauma center. Her name has not been released pending notification of family.

Several passengers aboard were treated at the scene by Rivergate Fire Department paramedics; two were transported to area hospitals for further evaluation, according to the department. At least one other rider was uninjured and remained on scene to speak with investigators.

“Our officers arrived within minutes of the initial 911 call and immediately began rendering aid alongside Fire personnel,” said RPD spokesperson Sgt. Julian Ackermann in a briefing outside the department's Rivergate Plaza headquarters shortly after sunrise. “The pedestrian sustained very serious injuries and was flown out by medical helicopter. We are asking the public to keep her and her family in their thoughts.”

Ackermann confirmed that the operator of the bus — whom the department declined to name pending arraignment — was administered a preliminary breath test at the scene and subsequently arrested on suspicion of driving under the influence. A separate evidentiary blood sample was drawn under standard procedure, Ackermann said, and the results will be forwarded to the county crime laboratory.

“Impaired driving is dangerous in any vehicle,” Ackermann added. “When it involves a large transit vehicle with members of the public on board and pedestrians in the roadway, the potential for tragedy is enormous. We take those cases with the utmost seriousness.”

South Main Street between Second and Fourth Avenues was closed for roughly six hours while the department's Traffic Investigation Unit documented the scene, photographed vehicle positions and skid evidence, and coordinated with City of Rivergate public works crews to secure the damaged light standard and restore power to the intersection. The roadway reopened to traffic before the morning rush.

ACTD response

Reached by phone Saturday morning, an ACTD spokesperson confirmed that a bus operating in Owl service was involved in the collision and said the District is cooperating fully with the Rivergate Police Department's investigation.

“Our first concern is for the pedestrian and for the riders who were on board, and we are in contact with their families to offer whatever support we can,” the spokesperson said in a statement. “The operator has been removed from service pending the outcome of the criminal investigation and our own internal safety review. Safety is the foundation of everything we do, and any conduct inconsistent with that standard will be addressed.”

The spokesperson declined to answer questions about the operator's tenure, training, or prior record, citing personnel confidentiality and the ongoing investigation. ACTD said a formal internal incident report would be completed in the coming weeks.

Owl service — the District's late-night route network — operates six routes between 11 p.m. and 5 a.m. daily. The bus involved in the collision was in service on one of those routes at the time.

At the scene

By mid-morning Saturday, the shattered globe of the City light standard still lay on the sidewalk beside a scarred concrete base, cordoned off with yellow caution tape. A pair of shoes and a scattering of personal items had been marked with numbered evidence tents earlier in the morning before being collected.

Nearby merchants said the stop is a regular one for overnight workers heading home.

“A lot of the folks who use that stop this time of night are coming off closing shifts — restaurant people, retail people,” said the owner of a 24-hour diner a block north on Main, who asked that his business not be named. “It's usually pretty quiet out there at two in the morning. You don't expect something like this.”

Another downtown resident, walking past the taped-off intersection with a cup of coffee, said she had been awakened by the sound of the impact. “It was a loud crack, then sirens for a long time,” she said. “I didn't know what had happened until I came out this morning.”

What happens next

The operator is expected to be booked into the Los Angeles County jail system pending arraignment, RPD said. Formal charges will be determined by the County District Attorney's Office once the investigation file, including the pending laboratory blood-alcohol analysis, is submitted for review. Under California law, driving under the influence causing injury is chargeable as a felony.

Anyone who witnessed the collision or who was in the immediate area of South Main Street and Third Avenue between approximately 1:45 a.m. and 2:15 a.m. Saturday is asked to contact the Rivergate Police Department's Traffic Investigation Unit at (626) 555-0111.

This is a developing story. The Sentinel will update this article as additional information becomes available, including the identities of the operator and the injured pedestrian once official releases are made.
